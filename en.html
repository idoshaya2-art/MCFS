About the survey — in plain terms

The survey mapped equipment gaps in the Ground Forces since October 7, 2023. Commanders at the battalion and company level, from every division, reported which equipment items their unit was missing and how those gaps were filled.

The goal of the survey is to translate into data-based numbers a pattern that emerged from the field: a large share of the critical equipment was supplied through civilian donations rather than through the military supply chain. The data is meant to help decision-makers gauge the size of the gap, where it is concentrated, and what it means for readiness ahead of the next conflict.

How the survey was built — the method in brief
Full transparency on how the data was collected
1
Targeted distribution. A structured questionnaire was sent to 225 combat units in the Ground Forces, through dedicated points of contact in each division.
2
Reporting by the command level. Each commander reported the equipment gaps in their unit on Oct 7, how they were filled, and how much they relied on donations (a 1–5 scale).
3
Quantified across 16 equipment categories. For each item, the level of shortage was marked: "no shortage" / "up to 30%" / "up to 60%" / "up to 90%" / "100% missing".
4
Qualitative evidence. 175 commanders added a concrete example of "life-saving equipment", and 115 agreed to a follow-up in-depth interview.
The findings are based on the respondents’ reports in the survey and reflect the picture as it emerged from the field. They are not a direct cross-check against the full system-wide inventory and supply data, but rather a basis for identifying trends, gap hotspots, and implications that warrant further examination.

The findings point to a consistent structural gap. The reserve force, which is the core of ground strength in a multi-front war, enters combat with significantly higher equipment gaps — 2–3 times — than the regular force, precisely in the life-saving items. In the next emergency call-up, without dedicated equipment standards and stock for the reserves, the scenario is likely to repeat.
